# TAMUZ — Digital Census Project

TAMUZ is a digital history project that returns to the original household schedules of the 1897 Census of the Russian Empire.

Instead of relying only on published statistical tables, the project works with surviving handwritten census sheets in order to recover individual- and household-level information, compare original records with published figures, and explore combinations of variables that were not preserved in the printed census.

## Project website

This repository contains the public website for the TAMUZ project.

The site presents:

- the research concept behind the project;
- the use of handwritten text recognition for historical census forms;
- the Transkribus model-training workflow;
- examples of research questions explored with the data;
- information on migration, household structures, Jewish households, and published census categories;
- a contact form for collaboration and research enquiries.

## Research workflow

The project combines:

- digitized census schedules;
- handwritten text recognition in Transkribus;
- manually corrected ground-truth transcriptions;
- Python-based data cleaning and structuring;
- validation against the original documents;
- historical and computational analysis.

A project-specific HTR model reduced the character error rate from 32.92% to 9.75%.

## Research themes

Current and completed analyses include:

- migration to Demiivka, a rapidly changing suburb of Kyiv;
- household size and generational composition;
- co-residence and marital status;
- female-headed households;
- occupational combinations;
- Jewish households as a focused case study;
- comparison between published census statistics and original household schedules.
